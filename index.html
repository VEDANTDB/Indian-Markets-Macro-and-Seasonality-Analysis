z = (reading − centre) ÷ scale. Credit, PMI and CPI use the report's own thresholds (credit 8–14%, PMI 50–54, CPI 2–6% band). The other centres and scales are my assumptions, so edit them to match your own 36-month history. Yield slope is inverted in the model.

Why seasonality exists in India

Jan–Feb: Union Budget speculation, then post-budget profit taking. Mar: advance-tax outflows and fiscal year-end NAV adjustments squeeze liquidity. Apr–May: fresh mandates and Q4 results support an "April effect" for banks, autos and IT. Jun–Aug: monsoon progress drives rural income, so autos, staples and fertilisers react; July is strong for pharma and defensives. Oct–Dec: Kharif harvest, Dussehra and Diwali lift retail lending, vehicles and housing, and it is the year's strongest window (autos, metals, energy, banks).

How the model works

1. Regime detectionEight indicators are converted to rolling 36-month z-scores. G = 0.35 Credit + 0.25 PMI + 0.20 IIP + 0.20 Earnings revisions. I = 0.35 CPI + 0.30 Repo + 0.20 Crude + 0.15 inverted yield slope. Regime 1: G≥0, I<0. Regime 2: G>0, I≥0. Regime 3: G≤0, I>0. Regime 4: G<0, I≤0. This dashboard takes raw readings and converts them with fixed centres and scales (editable under Calibration) because the 36-month history is not bundled.
2. Portfolio constructionBlack-Litterman blends market-implied returns with the regime view (risk aversion λ=2.85, τ=0.035). Hierarchical Risk Parity clusters correlated sectors so volatile ones (Metals, Realty) don't dominate risk. A quadratic optimiser then sets the final weights.
3. Constraints and safeguardsSingle sector 2% to 25%. Tracking error vs Nifty 50 at most 4.5% a year. Turnover at most 30% a quarter with a 0.15% transaction penalty. Cash 1% to 10%, at least 6 sectors held. Rebalance quarterly, only if drift exceeds ±2.5%. Circuit breakers: 8% peak-to-trough drawdown moves 30% to overnight TREPS; a correlation break above 0.85 signals a regime break.
4. Costs and caveatsRebalancing costs include brokerage, 18% GST on fees, and 0.1% STT on delivery trades, plus short-term capital gains tax for non-fund investors. Real cycles overlap: oil can spike while domestic growth is fine, and monsoon shocks can lift CPI without signalling a slowdown.

Glossary

Z-score: distance from average in standard deviations. Repo: RBI's policy rate; loans are linked to it via EBLR. EBLR: External Benchmark Lending Rate. IIP: Index of Industrial Production. PMI: Purchasing Managers' Index (50 = no change). NIM: bank net interest margin. CAD: current account deficit. Tracking error: how far returns stray from the index. Sharpe: return above risk-free per unit of volatility. HRP: Hierarchical Risk Parity. TREPS: overnight cash instrument.

Data caveats: the report's weight columns do not sum to 100, Regime 1's Bank weight (30%) exceeds its own 25% cap, and FMCG's FX elasticity (+0.39) looks misaligned. All three are flagged where they appear. Seasonality: the study does not state its sample period, win rates are historical tendencies rather than forecasts, and Energy in July is scored +0.1 although the month table lists it as an underperformer.
